## What I am least sure of

**1. Whether an 8 mm far-side bias is the right amount, or even the right fix.** The mechanism is a frozen wrist meeting a random yaw, so the *ideal* fix (rotate the fingers to align with the slot) is unavailable to the policy and unexpressible here. My term asks for the second-best thing: grasp/descend biased to the far side of cubeA and lift higher before translating. If the actual collision in most episodes is the **hand body** (not a fingertip) sweeping laterally, an 8 mm tcp offset may be too small to matter and the term will read as a constant discount inside the region — monotone and harmless, but not useful. `_SIDE_HALF` and `_H_CLEAR` are the two knobs I would sweep first if the trained residual shows no change in cubeB displacement.

**2. Grasp robustness versus clearance.** The stage-A optimum sits at a lateral offset of ~8 mm from cubeA's centre. If the frozen base policy's grasp is marginal in tight configs, an 8 mm residual nudge could tip it into grasp failure, and the reward would then be paying for a pose that loses the +3 rung. I believe the rung gap dominates (1.84 with a clear approach versus 3.0+ for any grasp), but this is the term I most expect to need re-weighting downward (`_W_INTRUSION` 0.35 → 0.2).

**3. What an optimiser exploits first.** My prediction: **`clear` inside stage B**. Lifting very high makes `height_gate = 0` and removes the penalty while `place` is still partly paid, so a residual could learn "lift higher than necessary and dawdle" at ~4.x/step instead of committing to the descent. It cannot exceed 5.0 that way and the stack is worth 6–8, but over 200 steps a timid policy that never descends is not obviously punished. Second candidate: `not_over` — the penalty vanishes at 25 mm of cubeA–cubeB horizontal separation, so a policy could park cubeA just inside 25 mm of cubeB and then intrude freely; the placement terms make that unattractive but the loophole exists.

**4. Stateless limitation, stated plainly (hacking.md #7).** "Did the hand shove cubeB during this episode?" is a history quantity. I cannot see cubeB's initial pose, cannot integrate its displacement, and cannot know the step index. I shape the instantaneous geometry that causes the shove, which means an episode where cubeB was knocked away in the first 20 steps and everything afterwards looks locally clean will score no worse than one that never touched it. That is a real gap between the reward and the failure metric, and it is the strongest argument for judging this reward by the trained residual's cubeB-displacement statistics rather than by the reward's own curves.

## What I predict validation will find

* **Hand-built states.** Completed stack 8.00, highest by ≥1.25. Held-above-and-never-released ≤6.75 (exactly at the stacked pose) or ~4.0 (held 5 cm high). Beside-the-target-on-the-table ≤2.0. Target knocked away with cubeA grasped ~3.0–3.5. Stacked wrong way round ≤2.0 (`z_offset = −0.04`, so `is_cubeA_on_cubeB` is false and it falls to stage A). Cube below the table at the right horizontal position ~0.0–0.2 (large `d_A`). All should pass.
* **Monotone sweeps.** Away from the goal horizontally in a grasped state: `place` falls and `not_over` rises, so both components push down — passes. Vertically up from the stacked pose: `place` falls, `not_over` stays 0 — passes. The residual risk is a sweep run in a state where the tcp is **not** near the goal and `is_cubeA_grasped` is false: stage A is then `2·reach·clear`, which rises if the sweep happens to move cubeA *towards* a distant tcp. The built-in reward has exactly this property (`2(1−tanh(5·d_tcp,A))`) and I did not fix it, because any reach term must depend on cubeA's position. If the harness sweeps from a stacked state with the hand at the stack, the distance to the tcp grows monotonically and this does not fire.
* **100 frozen-policy episodes.** I expect my reward to separate placed from not-placed more sharply than the built-in for two reasons that are not "grasping predicts everything": the grasp floor is 3 rather than 4 (so grasp-and-hold accumulates less relative to a stack), and `place` has a 20-gain component that actually distinguishes a 5 mm miss from a hit, where the built-in's `tanh(5·d)` is flat there. Where I am less confident is the *clearance* half: `clear` is bounded at 0.35 of the in-stage shaping, so if the base policy's intrusion behaviour is nearly identical across succeeded and failed episodes (i.e. both intrude, one gets lucky), the clearance term will contribute little episode-level separation and the win will come mostly from the finer placement gradient. I would rather report that honestly than claim the clearance term is doing work it may not be doing.
* **Sampler check.** ~0.7% of nominal draws fall in my tight core and ~2% in the full region; my sampler puts ~88% of rows in the core, i.e. roughly a 100× concentration. If the checker measures "fraction of my draws with face gap ≤ 12 mm" it should read ~0.88; if it measures against a centre-to-centre threshold instead of a face gap it will read much lower, because my rows sit at `d ≈ 0.059–0.080` with corner-first yaw and that is the whole point of the distinction.
